FnOnce ownership / normal-return destruction ledger
The original closure does not spell move, but its body invokes create_fn as FnOnce by value; it therefore captures ownership of create_fn. The general method's closure does the same. Candidate's extra forwarding call transfers F into the general method; it does not introduce another owning operation closure or retain a duplicate F in the outer wrapper. Borrowed self/name and the bool exclusive add no owning Drop payload.
Early as_dir/name error: operation_fn has not been consumed. Its owned create_fn capture is destroyed as with_locked_child's parameter when that helper returns, with no namespace guard acquired; same in both shapes.
lookup error after guard acquisition: helper locals, including namespace guard and dir_inode, are destroyed before the still-unconsumed operation_fn parameter/capture. Same helper and local order in both. No candidate local is successfully bound on the failing lookup path.
Positive-child rejection: operation_fn IS invoked and consumes its closure. Its uninvoked create_fn capture is destroyed as that operation closure returns AlreadyExists, while with_locked_child still owns the namespace guard and candidate. This happens in BOTH original and general exclusive implementation. Unlike the rejected delete helper, original does not keep create_fn as an untouched outer parameter on this branch. Do not assert that rejected callbacks must always drop after unlocking.
Callback error / callback success: invoking create_fn consumes it; its captures naturally drop as that call completes, while parent namespace guard remains held. Callback error skips postcheck and clone; callback success performs postcheck and possibly clone. Same order and owning closure scope in both. After operation returns, helper's local candidate is dropped before namespace guard, then dir_inode; a successful returned clone preserves a strong reference independently.
Adapter runs only after lookup_or_create_with has returned and therefore after with_locked_child's guard/local destruction. It captures no user callback or other owned environment. No callback survives to be newly destroyed by this adapter. These are source-derived normal-return relationships, not a runtime Drop trace or a complete panic/unwind proof.
