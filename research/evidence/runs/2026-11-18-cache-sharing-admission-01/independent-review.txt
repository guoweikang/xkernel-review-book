Why the live-hit difference matters, and what remains risk
Source shows Dentry owns Arc<DentryInner>, not a trivial borrowed token. Dropping a rejected final strong reference may destroy inner inode/location/children ownership and transitively release further objects. String destruction may release its buffer (subject to actual allocation). Changing their relative destruction/deallocation order therefore prevents claiming exact preservation merely from identical returned winner/map state. Actual final-reference status, allocator effects and observable consequences for real callers are NOT established here. No observed deadlock, use-after-free, allocator regression or kernel error is claimed. Both drops remain outside children guard, unlike the earlier delete-callback case. Missing acceptability proof is not proof that this concrete timing difference is harmful.
